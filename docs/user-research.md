# User Research — Helping Hand Robot

## Research Goal

Understand whether wheelchair following, voice-command control, and object retrieval assistance represents a real, unsolved problem for wheelchair users, disabled students, and caregivers — and whether a robot like this would actually be used.

---

## Interview Questions

These questions are designed for wheelchair users, disabled students, or their caregivers. Aim for 20–30 minute conversations. Listen for pain points they bring up unprompted.

1. Can you walk me through a typical day and where you rely on other people or tools for help?
2. What tasks do you wish you could do independently that currently require another person?
3. Have you ever needed to retrieve something from across a room or another area? How do you handle that today?
4. How do you feel about asking caregivers or people nearby for help — is that ever frustrating or embarrassing?
5. Have you used any assistive technology (apps, devices, robots) to help with mobility or daily tasks? What did you think of it?
6. If a robot could follow you around and carry things for you, what would your immediate concern be? (safety, noise, size, control?)
7. How comfortable are you with voice commands as an interface? Do you use voice assistants like Siri or Alexa today?
8. What environments do you spend the most time in — home, school, office, outdoor? Where would a robot helper be most useful?
9. What would make you trust a robot enough to use it around you regularly?
10. Is there anything about existing assistive tools that consistently frustrates or disappoints you?
